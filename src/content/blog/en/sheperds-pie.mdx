## Quick info

- **Servings:** 4
- **Total time:** about 1 hour 30 minutes
- **Prep:** 25 minutes
- **Cooking:** about 55-65 minutes
- **Resting:** 10-15 minutes
- **Tools:** food processor, large skillet (preferably 28cm/11 in), potato masher, 16 × 22cm (6 × 9 in) baking dish at least 5cm (2 in) deep

## Ingredients

### For the filling

- **Seitan:** 200g (7.1 oz)
- **Button mushrooms:** 150g (5.3 oz)
- **Carrot:** 100g (3.5 oz)
- **Onion:** 50g (1.8 oz)
- **Celery:** 30g (1.1 oz)
- **Frozen peas:** 80g (2.8 oz) (100g/3.5 oz if you like them more prominent)
- **Tomato passata:** 100g (3.5 oz)
- **Dry red wine:** 50g (1.8 oz)
- **Hot water:** 30g (1.1 oz) (plus more only if needed)
- **Soy sauce:** 2½ tablespoons (about 38g/1.3 oz)
- **Extra virgin olive oil:** 20g (0.7 oz) (10g/0.4 oz for the mushrooms, 10g/0.4 oz for the sauté)
- **Bay leaves:** 2
- **Chopped fresh rosemary:** ½ heaping teaspoon (or ¼ teaspoon dried)
- **Black pepper:** 8 turns of the pepper mill (2 per serving)
- **Salt:** 1 pinch (only if needed, after tasting)

### For the mash

- **Potatoes:** 615g (21.7 oz)
- **Oat milk:** 200-250g (7.1-8.8 oz) (unsweetened and unflavored)
- **Extra virgin olive oil:** 20g (0.7 oz)
- **Vegan grated cheese:** 20g (0.7 oz)
- **Salt:** scant ½ teaspoon (adjust after tasting)

## Method

### Make the filling

1. **Cook the potatoes:** put the 615g (21.7 oz) of whole, unpeeled potatoes in a pot and cover them with cold water. Bring to a boil and cook for about 30-40 minutes, depending on their size: they're done when a fork slides easily all the way to the center. Meanwhile, make the filling.

2. **Chop the mushrooms:** clean the 150g (5.3 oz) of button mushrooms, trimming off the earthy end of the stems, then chop them roughly and pulse them in the food processor. They should end up quite fine, but not pureed. Transfer them to a bowl.

3. **Chop the seitan and vegetables:** roughly chop the 200g (7.1 oz) of seitan, 100g (3.5 oz) of carrot, 50g (1.8 oz) of onion and 30g (1.1 oz) of celery, then pulse them in the food processor until fine and fairly even, similar to the texture of ground meat. Don't process it into a paste.

4. **Cook the mushrooms:** heat 10g (0.4 oz) of oil in a large skillet, preferably 28cm (11 in), over medium-high heat. Add the chopped mushrooms, spread them across the bottom and cook, stirring occasionally, until they release their water and it evaporates almost completely. Keep cooking for another 1-2 minutes, until they start to lightly brown.

5. **Add the chopped mix and the aromatics:** lower the heat to medium and add the remaining 10g (0.4 oz) of oil, all the seitan-and-vegetable mixture, the 2 bay leaves and ½ heaping teaspoon of chopped fresh rosemary. Stir well to distribute the oil and aromatics, then cook for about 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables soften and the mixture starts to take on some color.

6. **Deglaze with the wine:** raise the heat to medium-high and pour in 50g (1.8 oz) of dry red wine. Stir right away, gently scraping the bottom of the pan to release the browned bits, and cook for about 2-3 minutes, until the liquid has reduced almost completely and the sharp smell of wine is gone.

7. **Add the peas:** lower the heat back to medium, add 80g (2.8 oz) of frozen peas (100g/3.5 oz if you like them more prominent) and stir for about 2 minutes.

8. **Finish the seasoning:** add 100g (3.5 oz) of tomato passata, 2½ tablespoons of soy sauce, 30g (1.1 oz) of hot water and 8 turns of the pepper mill. Stir well to distribute everything evenly.

9. **Cook the filling:** lower the heat to medium-low and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally. The filling should turn soft and moist, with no pooled liquid at the bottom. If it dries out too much before the vegetables are cooked through, add 1 tablespoon of hot water at a time. At the end, remove the 2 bay leaves, taste and add a pinch of salt only if needed.

### Make the mash

1. **Mash the potatoes:** once the potatoes are cooked, drain them and let them cool just enough to handle. Peel them while they're still warm and mash them with a potato masher directly into a small saucepan.

2. **Make the mash creamy:** add 200g (7.1 oz) of oat milk, 20g (0.7 oz) of extra virgin olive oil and a scant ½ teaspoon of salt. Stir over low heat until smooth and creamy.

3. **Stir in the cheese:** add 20g (0.7 oz) of vegan grated cheese and stir until fully incorporated. If the mash is still too stiff, gradually add up to another 50g (1.8 oz) of oat milk, stirring after each addition: it should be creamy but hold its shape, so that when you drag a spoon through it, the trail stays visible without closing up right away. Taste and adjust the salt if needed.

### Assemble and bake the Shepherd's Pie

1. **Turn on the oven,** conventional (static) mode, to 200°C (392°F) and let it preheat.

2. **Spread the filling:** pour it into a 16 × 22cm (6 × 9 in) baking dish, at least 5cm (2 in) deep, and level it out with a spoon without pressing down too much. No need to grease the dish.

3. **Cover with the mash:** add it by the spoonful, spreading it over the entire surface of the filling. Start from the edges and work toward the center — it'll be easier to spread without dragging the filling underneath.

4. **Finish the surface:** gently level the mash with the back of a spoon, making sure it completely covers the filling. Then run the tines of a fork over the surface to create small ridges and peaks that will help it brown nicely.

5. **Bake:** cook on the center rack of the preheated oven at 200°C (392°F) for about 30 minutes, until the top starts to turn golden and the filling bubbles gently around the edges. If the mash is still pale after 30 minutes, move the dish to the upper part of the oven and bake for another 5 minutes, keeping an eye on it so it doesn't burn.

6. **Let it rest:** take it out of the oven and let it rest for 10-15 minutes before serving. This lets the filling settle and makes it much easier to serve without it falling apart.

## Notes

**Storage:** keep the Shepherd's Pie in the fridge, in an airtight container, for 2-3 days.

**Freezing:** you can freeze it after baking, ideally already portioned. Let it cool, transfer it to freezer-safe containers and eat it within 2-3 months for best results.

**Reheating:** thaw the portions in the fridge, then reheat them in a conventional oven at 180°C (356°F) for about 15-20 minutes, until hot all the way through. If the mash looks like it's drying out, cover it with foil for the first part of reheating.
