For roast potatoes, it's best to use old, starchy potatoes. They've got more starch: crispy on the outside, soft in the middle. New potatoes work too, but they tend to stay denser.

This is the simple version: you cut, you season, and straight into the oven.


## Quick info

- Servings: 2 generous servings
- Total time: about 1 hour
- Prep: 10 min
- Cook: 50 min
- Tools:
    * baking pan
    * knife
    * spatula or turner

- Notes:
    * Don't overlap the potatoes: they need to sit in a single layer.
    * Better to turn them with a spatula, not a fork, which breaks them up.

## Ingredients

- 600g (21.2 oz) starchy potatoes
- 4 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil (2 for seasoning, 2 halfway through cooking)
- 3 tablespoons water
- 2 unpeeled garlic cloves
- 1 sprig rosemary
- ¾ teaspoon fine salt (adjust to your taste: you can add more at the end of cooking)
- ¾ teaspoon pink peppercorns

## Method

1. **Preheat the oven** to 200°C (392°F) conventional (180°C/356°F convection).
2. **Wash and dry the 600g (21.2 oz) of potatoes.** If the skin looks good, you can leave it on. Cut them into fairly even wedges:
    * medium potatoes → into 4
    * large potatoes → into 6
3. **Season them in the pan:** put the potatoes in the baking pan and add:
    * 2 tablespoons of oil
    * 2 garlic cloves
    * the rosemary
    * ¾ teaspoon of salt
    * ¾ teaspoon of pink peppercorns, crushed between your fingers as you sprinkle them

    Mix well with your hands so every potato gets coated in oil, then pour 3 tablespoons of water into the pan.
4. **Bake in the middle of the oven** for about 45 minutes.
5. **Turn the potatoes** with a spatula, baste them with the cooking juices, and add the remaining 2 tablespoons of oil. Move the pan to the top of the oven, raise the temperature to 220°C (428°F), and cook for another 10 minutes. Taste and add a little more salt if needed.
6. **If you want extra crispiness, finish under the broiler (grill):** raise the temperature to 250°C (482°F), turn on the broiler, and leave for 5–6 minutes, until deep golden brown.
